Jamie: Here's the thing nobody talks about: deep work requires sustained attention. Every time you switch contexts, there's a cognitive cost. Research shows it can take 15-20 minutes to fully re-engage with complex work after an interruption.

Jamie: You never actually reach deep focus. You're perpetually in that warm-up phase. It's like trying to boil water but turning down the heat every few minutes. The water never boils.

Jamie: We implemented "focus blocks." Everyone has at least two hours each morning where they're not expected to respond to anything unless it's literally on fire. No meetings, no Slack, nothing.

Jamie: Honestly? Initial resistance. People felt like they were being "bad team players." But after two weeks, productivity metrics went up. And more importantly, people reported feeling less stressed and more accomplished.

Jamie: We defined what "urgent" actually means. Turns out, almost nothing is actually urgent in the "needs a response in the next 2 hours" sense. We have a system: if something truly can't wait, there's a specific channel for emergencies. It gets used maybe once a month.

Alex: So it's about setting clear expectations.

Jamie: Exactly. And trusting people. We trust our team to check their messages at reasonable intervals. We don't need instant responses. What we need is thoughtful work.

Jamie: Exactly. Culture is what you do, not what you say.
